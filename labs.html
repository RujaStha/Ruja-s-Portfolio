Lab Experiments

🔌 Experiment 1: Verification of Ohm’s Law

Aim:

To verify Ohm’s Law by studying the relationship between voltage and current in an electrical circuit.

Apparatus/Materials:

Battery, resistor, ammeter, voltmeter, rheostat, key, connecting wires.

Description & Result:

In this experiment, a circuit was set up with a resistor connected to a battery. The ammeter was connected in series to measure current, while the voltmeter was connected in parallel across the resistor to measure voltage. The rheostat was used to vary the current. Multiple readings of voltage and current were recorded by adjusting the rheostat. A graph of voltage (V) versus current (I) was plotted, which produced a straight line passing through the origin. This showed a direct proportionality between voltage and current.

Conclusion:

Ohm’s Law was verified, proving that at constant temperature, current is directly proportional to voltage (V ∝ I).

 What I Learned:

I learned proper circuit connections, safe handling of electrical equipment, accurate data recording, and graph plotting. The experiment improved my understanding of resistance and basic electrical principles.



🧪 Experiment 2: Acid–Base Titration

Aim:

To determine the concentration of an acid or base using titration.

Apparatus/Materials:

Burette, pipette, conical flask, stand, funnel, indicator (phenolphthalein/methyl orange), standard solution.

Description & Result:

A measured volume of acid/base was taken in a conical flask using a pipette. A few drops of indicator were added. The other solution of known concentration was filled in a burette. The solution from the burette was slowly added to the flask while stirring continuously. The color of the indicator changed at the endpoint, indicating neutralization. The volume of solution used was noted and calculations were performed to find the unknown concentration.

Conclusion:

The experiment confirmed that acids and bases react in fixed proportions during neutralization.

 What I Learned:

I learned accurate measurement techniques, careful handling of glassware, and how chemical reactions are quantitatively analyzed in laboratories.



🧂 Experiment 3: Preparation of Salt

Aim:

To prepare a pure sample of salt through a chemical reaction.

Apparatus/Materials:

Beaker, glass rod, evaporating dish, filter paper, funnel, burner.

Description & Result:

An acid and a base were reacted in a beaker to form salt and water. The solution was filtered to remove impurities. The clear filtrate was heated in an evaporating dish until crystals began to form. The solution was cooled to allow crystallization. Pure salt crystals were collected and dried.

Conclusion:

Crystallization is an effective method for obtaining pure solid substances from solutions.

 What I Learned:

I learned about purification methods, filtration, evaporation, and crystallization processes used in chemical industries.
