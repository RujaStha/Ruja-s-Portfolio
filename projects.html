🔬 Project 1: Hydraulic Lift Model

Aim:

To demonstrate Pascal’s Law and understand how pressure is transmitted through liquids.

Apparatus/Materials:

Two plastic syringes, rubber tube, water, cardboard base, tape.

Description & Result:

In this project, two syringes were connected using a rubber tube filled with water. When pressure was applied to the smaller syringe, the plunger of the larger syringe moved upward and was able to lift a small weight placed on it. This showed that pressure applied at one point in a confined liquid is transmitted equally in all directions. The system worked smoothly without air bubbles, ensuring accurate pressure transmission.

Conclusion:

The experiment successfully demonstrated Pascal’s Law. Even a small force applied to a smaller area can lift a heavier load when transmitted through a liquid.

 What I Learned:

I learned how hydraulic systems work in real-life machines like car brakes, lifts, and heavy construction equipment. This project improved my understanding of pressure, force, and fluid mechanics, and helped me connect theory with practical application.



🧪 Project 2: pH Testing of Household Substances

Aim:

To determine whether common household substances are acidic, basic, or neutral.

Apparatus/Materials:

pH paper/indicator, lemon juice, soap solution, milk, water, vinegar.

Description & Result:

Small samples of different substances were tested using pH paper. The color change on the pH strip was compared with the color chart. Lemon juice and vinegar showed acidic pH values, soap solution showed a basic pH, and water was neutral. Each substance displayed a different level of acidity or basicity.

Conclusion:

The pH scale is an effective way to classify substances and understand their chemical nature.

 What I Learned:

I understood how acids and bases are present in daily life and why maintaining proper pH is important for health, agriculture, and the environment. The project improved my observation and recording skills.



⚡ Project 3: Simple Electric Motor

Aim:

To demonstrate how electrical energy is converted into mechanical energy.

Apparatus/Materials:

Battery, copper wire coil, magnet, safety pins, rubber band, connecting wires.

Description & Result:

A coil of copper wire was suspended between two supports connected to a battery. A magnet was placed beneath the coil. When current flowed through the coil, it began to rotate due to the interaction between the magnetic field and electric current. The rotation continued as long as the circuit was complete.

Conclusion:

The experiment proved that a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field experiences a force, producing motion.

 What I Learned:

I understood the working principle of electric motors used in fans, mixers, and electric vehicles. It strengthened my knowledge of electromagnetism.
